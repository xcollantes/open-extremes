Do stocks reveal their daily low or high in the first X minutes of trading?

If you could tell early in the day where a stock's daily low or high will be, you could trade on it: buy at the low, sell at the high, and ride the rest of the session on a prediction you can depend on.

WhyHypothesisMethodResultsConclusionLimitationsReproduce

Why test this

Watch a stock for a few weeks and its low or high for the day often seems to come right after the open. If that were reliable, it would make for a clean trade at 09:50 ET: buy with a stop just under the morning's low, or sell short with a stop just above its high. You know your risk before you enter.

Before trading it, I wanted to know three things: how often a stock's low or high really is in by 09:50, how that compares with the market as a whole, and how often it would happen if prices moved at random.

Hypothesis

Stocks reveal their daily low or high early: within the first X minutes of trading, the day's low or the day's high is set on at least 60% of days.

X isn't fixed in advance. The test looks for the earliest X that gives a 60% reading. 60% is an arbitrary benchmark, not a number from theory or past results.

  • The test uses eight stocks, picked by hand: NVDA, TSLA, AAPL, GOOGL, CRWD, ORCL, INTC and PLTR.
  • SPY and QQQ are the benchmarks. They track the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq-100, so no single company drives them. If the stocks look like them, the pattern belongs to the market, not to single stocks.
  • "Low or high" counts a day if either extreme is set in the window. To trade it you need to know which one, so the low and the high are also measured on their own.
  • 09:50 ET (X = 20) is a fixed reference point for comparing with chance, year by year, and for the trade.

Method

Period
Jan 3, 2023 to Sep 30, 2026
Days
931 full sessions per symbol
Bars
1-minute, Alpaca SIP feed
Stocks
NVDA, TSLA, AAPL, GOOGL, CRWD, ORCL, INTC, PLTR
Benchmarks
SPY, QQQ
Chance model
20,000 simulated days per symbol

For each day I find the first 1-minute bar that touches the day's low, and the first that touches its high. If two bars tie, the earlier one counts. Then, for every X from 1 to 390 minutes, I count the share of days whose low or high was set within the first X minutes, and find the first X where that share reaches 60%. Only full 09:30 to 16:00 sessions are used. The stock average is the plain mean of the eight stocks' results.

What chance looks like

Chance isn't 20 out of 390 minutes. Random price paths put their lows and highs near the start or the end of the day more often than in the middle (the arcsine law). The open is also the most volatile part of the session, and bigger early moves pull both extremes even closer to the open.

So the baseline is a set of random days whose minute-by-minute volatility follows each symbol's own pattern. These paths have no direction you could predict. Beating them means the open does something that volatility alone doesn't explain. The gap between the real rate and this baseline is the excess, and z is that gap divided by its standard error. For a test chosen in advance, z above 2 is unlikely to be noise.

Considerations

  • A real effect should show up in every year, not in one stretch of the market.
  • The SIP feed covers every US exchange. IEX data alone sees a small slice of volume and can put the day's high and low in the wrong minute.
  • Picking the X that looks best can turn up z near 3 on pure noise. So the chance comparisons use the fixed 09:50 reference, not the X that looked best.

At the fixed 09:50 reference, all eight stocks have their low or high in on more than 60% of days, 74.4% on average. Neither benchmark did: QQQ reached 54.0% and SPY 44.9%. Each stock also beat its own chance rate, by 5 to 10 points (8.8 on average), about twice QQQ's gap and four times SPY's. But chance alone clears 60% for every stock (63% to 69%), so the 60% line says little by itself. Taken alone, the low and the high fall well short: 39.8% and 37.4% for the average stock.

Chance differs by symbol because each one's volatility is spread across the day differently. The benchmarks have a calmer open relative to the rest of the day, so their chance rate is lower.

3. Best performers

  • Low or high: CRWD reaches 60% first, at 7 minutes, then GOOGL at 8 and PLTR and INTC at 9. At 09:50 the order is CRWD (78.6%), PLTR (76.5%), GOOGL (76.4%) and INTC (75.2%).
  • Low alone: CRWD (10:35), PLTR (10:51) and GOOGL (10:55) reach 60% first. At 09:50, CRWD's low is closest to the mark at 45.3%, still 15 points short.
  • High alone: TSLA (11:17) and PLTR (11:18) reach 60% first. No stock's high is near 60% at 09:50, and the best is PLTR at 39.1%.
  • Against chance, the order changes. CRWD and PLTR have the most volatile opens, so chance alone gives them high rates (69.4% and 67.9%). By the gap over chance at 09:50, GOOGL (+10.2 points) and INTC (+10.0) lead, while CRWD (+9.3) and PLTR (+8.6) sit in the middle.

Conclusion

Passes as worded, but it doesn't give a usable prediction.

  • Every stock's low or high is in on 60% of days within the first 7 to 11 minutes, and by 09:50 on 71% to 79%, in every year. QQQ needs 26 minutes and SPY 40.
  • Chance gets every stock to 60% only 5 to 7 minutes later, so the 60% reading proves little. What holds up is the gap over chance: about 9 points at 09:50 for the average stock, two to four times the benchmarks' gap. Early extremes are mostly a single-stock effect.
  • CRWD, PLTR and GOOGL are the strongest, with INTC close behind. Against chance, GOOGL and INTC lead.
  • When the 60% reading comes, you don't know which extreme is in. The low alone takes until 10:35 to 11:33 to reach 60%, and the high until 11:17 to 11:55.
  • The long trade's small profit came almost entirely from the stocks rising during the day.

The first few minutes tell you that one of the day's extremes is probably in, but not which one. Without that, there's no trade to place.

Limitations

  • The 60% benchmark is arbitrary, so the pass or fail verdict is too. The gap over chance doesn't depend on it.
  • No trading costs. Spreads, fees and slippage are ignored, and every stop fills exactly at its price. Treat the trade results as a best case.
  • X is found from the data rather than fixed in advance, so the times to 60% may look slightly better than they would on new data.
  • In-sample only. Every number comes from the same 931 days, and nothing has been checked on data after Oct 1, 2026.
  • Many comparisons. Ten symbols, three measures (low, high, either) and two trades add up to a lot of tests, so expect a few results to look stronger than they are.
  • Hand-picked stocks. All eight are large, heavily traded and mostly tech. Smaller or quieter stocks may behave differently.
  • The stocks move together. They share the same days and the same market, so the eight results aren't eight independent tests.
  • A rising market. SPY nearly doubled over the period, which flatters any trade that buys.
  • The chance model's volatility pattern is fit to the same days it's tested on. It sets the size of moves, not their direction, so it can't create an edge. It can absorb effects that come purely from when volatility happens.
  • Nothing known at the open is used. The overnight gap, news and earnings days could all change where the extremes land.
  • Early-close days, about three a year, are left out.

Next tests

  • Split days by the opening gap, to see whether it tells you which extreme is in. Gap-down days may set the low early, and gap-up days the high.
  • Repeat on a larger, randomly chosen set of stocks, including smaller ones.
  • Re-run on data after Oct 1, 2026, with X fixed at the times found here.
  • Add the bid-ask spread and stop slippage to the trade.

Reproduce

Every number here comes from notebooks/open_extremes.ipynb in the open-extremes repo. Section 5 of the notebook covers the eight stocks and the benchmarks. The repo includes the 1-minute bars for all ten symbols in notebooks/data/, so a rerun doesn't need API keys.

git clone https://github.com/xcollantes/open-extremes
cd open-extremes && uv sync
cd notebooks
uv run jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace open_extremes.ipynb

To pull other symbols or dates, copy .env.example to .env and add Alpaca keys.
