Do stocks reveal their daily low or high in the first X minutes of trading?

If you could tell early in the day where a stock's daily low or high will be, you could trade on it: buy at the low, sell at the high, and ride the rest of the session on a prediction you can depend on.

WhyHypothesesMethodResultsConclusionLimitationsReproduce

Why test this

Watch a stock for a few weeks and its low for the day often seems to come right after the open. If that were reliable for NVDA, it would make for a clean trade: buy at 09:50 ET and put a stop just under the 09:30 to 09:50 low. You know your risk before you enter.

Before trading it, I wanted two numbers: how often the low really is in by 09:50, and how often it would be if prices moved at random.

Hypotheses

The hypothesis named NVDA and the 20-minute window up front. Other windows, other symbols and the daily high are follow-ups.

  1. NVDA's daily low is set in the first 20 minutes on at least 80% of days.

    The strong claim, and the one the trade needs. It fails if the measured rate is clearly below 80%.

  2. The low lands in the first 20 minutes more often than chance.

    The weak claim: the open matters, even if not 80% of the time. It passes if the gap over chance is well above noise and shows up every year.

  3. Buying at 09:50 with a stop under the morning low makes money.

    Buy at the close of the 09:49 bar, place a stop just under the 09:30 to 09:50 low, and sell at 16:00. It passes if the average day is clearly positive.

Method

Period
Jan 3, 2023 to Sep 30, 2026
Days
931 full sessions per symbol
Bars
1-minute, Alpaca SIP feed
Stocks
NVDA, TSLA, AAPL, GOOGL, CRWD, ORCL, INTC, PLTR
Market controls
SPY, QQQ
Chance model
20,000 simulated days per symbol

For each day I find the first 1-minute bar that touches the day's low. "Low set in the first 20 minutes" means that bar started before 09:50 ET. If two bars tie, the earlier one counts. Only full 09:30 to 16:00 sessions are used.

What chance looks like

Chance isn't 20 out of 390 minutes. Random price paths put their lows near the start or the end of the day more often than in the middle (the arcsine law), so even a plain random walk sets its low early far more often than 5% of the time. The open is also the most volatile part of the session, and bigger early moves pull the low even closer to the open.

So the baseline is a set of random days whose minute-by-minute volatility follows each symbol's own pattern. These paths have no direction you could predict. Beating them means the open does something that volatility alone doesn't explain. The gap between the real rate and this baseline is the excess, and z is that gap divided by its standard error. For a test chosen in advance, z above 2 is unlikely to be noise.

Considerations

  • SPY and QQQ are index funds, so no single company drives them. If a stock's rate looks like theirs, the pattern belongs to the market, not the stock.
  • A real effect should show up in every year, not in one stretch of the market.
  • The SIP feed covers every US exchange. IEX data alone sees a small slice of volume and can put the day's high and low in the wrong minute.
  • Scanning all 390 windows can turn up z near 3 on pure noise, so a peak found by scanning is a lead, not a result.

Every stock beat its own chance rate at 09:50, by 3 to 10 points, and seven of the eight beat it by more than either index fund did. So early lows are mostly a single-stock effect rather than a market one. None of the stocks gets close to 80%. CRWD is highest at 45%.

Chance differs by symbol because each one's volatility is spread across the day differently. The index funds have a calmer open relative to the rest of the day, so their chance rate is lower.

On NVDA the trade averaged +0.11% a day before costs, but it lost money on most days. Buying at 09:50 and holding to the close with no stop made +0.074% a day, so most of the profit is NVDA drifting up during the day, which any 09:50 purchase would have caught. Conclusion

HypothesisVerdictEvidence
H1: low by 09:50 on 80% of daysRejected38.7% of days
H2: more often than chanceSupported+7.1 points, z = 4.7. Holds every year and in all eight stocks, and is weaker in SPY and QQQ.
H3: the 09:50 trade paysNot shown+0.11% a day before costs, mostly from NVDA rising during the day. The short side earned nothing.

Single stocks do set their lows early more often than chance would, and it's not a fluke of one year or one stock. But for NVDA that means 39% of days, not 80%, and the trade built on it earned little beyond what simply holding NVDA during the day earned. As tested, 09:50 isn't an entry point.

Limitations

  • No trading costs. Spreads, fees and slippage are ignored, and every stop fills exactly at its price. Treat the trade results as a best case.
  • In-sample only. Every number comes from the same 931 days, and nothing has been checked on data after Oct 1, 2026.
  • Many comparisons. Ten symbols, two sides of the day and 390 windows add up to a lot of tests, and the follow-up results carry that risk.
  • Hand-picked symbols. All ten are large, heavily traded and mostly tech. Smaller or quieter stocks may behave differently.
  • A rising market. SPY nearly doubled over the period, which flatters any trade that buys.
  • The chance model's volatility pattern is fit to the same days it's tested on. It sets the size of moves, not their direction, so it can't create an edge. It can absorb effects that come purely from when volatility happens.
  • Nothing known at the open is used. The overnight gap, news and earnings days could all change where the low lands.
  • Early-close days, about three a year, are left out.

Next tests

  • Split days by the opening gap. Gap-down days may set the low early more often, and gap-up days the high.
  • Re-run on data after Oct 1, 2026, with the 20-minute window fixed in advance.
  • Add the bid-ask spread and stop slippage to the trade.

Reproduce

Every number here comes from notebooks/open_extremes.ipynb in the open-extremes repo. The repo includes the 1-minute bars for all ten symbols in notebooks/data/, so a rerun doesn't need API keys.

git clone https://github.com/xcollantes/open-extremes
cd open-extremes && uv sync
cd notebooks
uv run jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace open_extremes.ipynb

To pull other symbols or dates, copy .env.example to .env and add Alpaca keys.
